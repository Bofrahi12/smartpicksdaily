When to redeem your points (and when to wait)

Smart Picks Daily · Rewards Playbook · For adults 18+ in the US

Every rewards balance asks the same quiet question: redeem now, or wait? It feels like a small decision, and most people answer it by ignoring it — the points sit there for months while life goes on. Points are not savings. Programs change their exchange rates, balances expire, companies close programs, and a balance that was worth $20 last year can be worth noticeably less this year. At the same time, redeeming too fast can waste value as well, because many programs run bonus redemption events where the same points buy more. The skill is knowing which situation you are in. This guide gives you the rule.

Why timing matters at all

Points are a promise, not money, and the program controls the promise. It can raise the number of points a reward costs, lower the value of a point, add new conditions, or close the program entirely — and its terms almost always allow it to do so. That is why a balance left sitting still tends to lose value over time, slowly and quietly. Expiry is the sharper version of the same risk: many programs cancel points that sit unused for 6 to 12 months, and some cancel a whole balance if the account goes inactive. Our guide to why points expire explains the expiry rules in detail, including the activity that usually resets the clock. The practical point is simple: points you have not redeemed are points you do not yet own, and the longer you wait without a reason, the more risk you carry for no benefit.

Redeem now when...

  • You have reached a payout threshold — if you can withdraw $10 or take a gift card today, take it. A redeemed reward is yours; a balance is a promise.
  • Your points are near expiry — check the expiry date first. If any points expire within the next month, redeem what you can now rather than losing them entirely.
  • The program has announced changes — a devaluation, a new fee, or a program closure is a clear signal to cash out while the old terms still apply.
  • You have a real use for the reward — a gift card for a shop you visit this week, or cash toward a bill, is worth more than points waiting for a perfect moment that never comes.
  • The balance is small — small balances gain nothing from waiting, and they are the first to be forgotten. Redeem them and start fresh.

Waiting can pay when...

Waiting is the right call only when there is a specific, dated reason to wait. The best reason is a bonus redemption event: many programs periodically offer extra value — a gift card worth 20 percent more, or a lower points price on a reward — for a limited window. If you know such an event is coming within a few weeks and your points are nowhere near expiry, waiting is rational. The second reason is a threshold: if you are a small step away from a better reward tier, and you will earn the remaining points through normal activity anyway, a short wait buys a better payout. The third is a planned purchase: saving points for a specific item you intend to buy next month is sensible, as long as the points will still be valid then. Notice what these three reasons share: a date, a number, and a plan. And when the choice is between a gift card and cash, our comparison of gift cards vs. cash will help you pick the payout that fits the moment.

The expiry trap

Expiry is where most points are lost, and it is almost always avoidable. The trap works like this: a program sets a 12-month expiry, you earn points steadily, and the oldest points quietly expire while you are still earning new ones. The balance looks healthy, but its oldest layer is rotting. The defence is a monthly check that takes two minutes: open each program you use, look at the balance and the earliest expiry date, and redeem anything that expires within the next 60 days. Some programs let a single small activity — one survey, one scan, one purchase — reset the expiry clock on the whole balance, which makes a tiny monthly action worth real money. Set a recurring reminder on the first of the month and the trap stops working on you. A balance you check monthly is a balance you control; a balance you never check belongs to the program.

A simple rule that covers both cases

Here is the rule in one sentence: redeem as soon as you reach a payout threshold, unless a named bonus event is coming within the next month and your points are safe until then. That is it. It protects you from devaluation and expiry, because your money spends most of its life redeemed rather than promised, and it still lets you capture the occasional bonus, because a dated event is a real reason to wait. Apply it to every program you use, and the timing question stops being a question. The points stop piling up in accounts you forget about, the rewards start arriving while they are still worth having, and the only thing left to decide is what to redeem for — which is the enjoyable part.

Quick questions

Do points lose value over time?

Yes, gradually. Programs can raise redemption prices, lower point values, or change terms, and many balances expire after 6 to 12 months of inactivity. Redeeming regularly protects you from all three.

What is a bonus redemption event?

A limited period when the same points buy more — for example, a gift card worth extra value, or a lower points price on a reward. If one is coming soon and your points are safe, waiting for it is usually worth it.

Should I ever let points expire?

No, if you can avoid it. Even a small redemption is worth more than points that vanish. Check expiry dates monthly and redeem anything close to expiring.
