Survey terms: 5 words to find first

Smart Picks Daily · Survey Guides · Scam Spotter · For adults 18+ in the US

Nobody reads the full terms of a survey site. Not us, not you, not — we suspect — most of the people who wrote them. But buried in those pages are five words that decide almost everything about your experience: whether you qualify, whether you get paid, when you can withdraw, when your balance evaporates and what happens to your information. Open the terms, search for these five words, and read the sentences around them. It takes three minutes and it is the core skill behind our Scam Spotter service.

1. “Eligibility”

This word controls who the panel wants and what counts as a valid account. Look for the age requirement (offers on this site are for adults 18+), the country rules — many panels are US-only or pay differently by region — and the one-account-per-person or per-household rule. That last one causes more lost payouts than almost anything else: two accounts in one household, or a second account after a forgotten first one, can mean both get closed at redemption time. Eligibility language also covers honesty rules. Panels compare your profile answers against survey answers, and an “eligibility” clause is usually where they reserve the right to remove points for inconsistent responses.

2. “Quota” (and its cousin, “screen”)

Surveys fill up by category: a study might need 200 parents, 150 non-parents, and then it is full. “Quota full” is the message you get when your category closes — sometimes two questions in, sometimes uncomfortably deep into a survey. The terms explain how the panel handles that moment. Better panels credit a small amount or enter you into a draw when you are screened out late; the terms will say so. If the quota language says incomplete and screened-out surveys earn nothing in all cases, expect frustration and plan your time accordingly. Screening itself is normal — research needs specific audiences. What you are checking is whether the panel treats your time with any respect when it happens.

3. “Threshold”

The redemption threshold is the minimum balance you must reach before withdrawing, and it is the most important number in the whole document. Pair it with the conversion rate — what the points are worth — and you can compute the honest size of the commitment: how many completed surveys stand between you and your first redemption. Our guide to how rewards online work shows the full calculation. Also read what sits near “threshold”: processing times for payouts, any fees, and whether gift cards and account payments carry different minimums. A program can be legitimate and still be a poor use of time because its threshold is set where few users ever arrive.

4. “Expiry” (and “inactivity”)

Points are a liability on a provider’s books, so most programs delete them on a schedule: after a fixed date, in the order earned, or when an inactivity clock runs out. Find the rule before you start building a balance, because it dictates your strategy — how many programs you can juggle and how quickly you should redeem. Our points-expiry guide turns this into a five-minute monthly routine. If you cannot find the word “expiry” anywhere in the terms or help pages, that is information too: rules that are hard to find are rarely generous.

5. “Data” (and “sharing” / “third parties”)

Survey panels are, by design, data businesses — your answers are the product. The question is what else travels with them. Read what the terms say about sharing: are responses anonymized and aggregated, or is identifiable information passed to partners? Look for email-sharing language too, because some panels sell or share your address with marketing partners, which is how one sign-up turns into a flooded inbox. This word is the reason our Scam Spotter checks exist: a panel whose offer page shouts about rewards while its data clauses quietly claim broad rights to your information has told you exactly what it values. Believe it, and use a separate email for anything you do join.

Three minutes, five searches, one decision

Eligibility, quota, threshold, expiry, data. Find them, read the surrounding lines, and you will understand a panel better than most of its members do. When we walk a new site for our picks, this search is step one — before the sign-up, before the test survey, before anything gets recommended. Offers that describe one thing on the landing page and another in these five clauses are the offers that never make our list. Now you can run the same check yourself.

Quick questions

What if a site has no visible terms?

Do not join it. A program asking for your data and time while publishing no terms at all has failed the most basic trust check there is.

Is being screened out a sign of a scam?

By itself, no — screening is how research works. The warning sign is a pattern: repeated late screen-outs with zero credit, on nearly every survey.

Which word matters most?

Threshold, because combined with the conversion rate it tells you the true distance to your first redemption. Expiry is a close second.
